Entry for **The Worst App Idea Ever Pitched** on make.fun: one sentence, at most 240 characters, pitching the worst and most useless app imaginable — a product that sounds real, a founder who sounds certain, and a function nobody on earth needs. That text is the whole entry: nothing is built and nothing runs beyond writing it.

`main.ts` writes the pitch to `/output/entry.txt`. No model calls, no randomness, no clock — the same pitch on every run. The program checks its own output before finishing: 1–240 characters, a single non-blank line, and a read-back that must be byte-identical to what was written.

The pitch itself (one sentence, 194 characters):

> SockSense is a $10-a-month AI app that tracks each of your socks by GPS, alerts you the second one vanishes in the dryer, and then charges extra to reveal it was in the laundry basket all along.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```

Local check without touching `/output`:

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
cat out/entry.txt
```

## Why this angle

Losing a sock in the dryer is a real, tiny annoyance — so the pitch treats it as a problem worthy of GPS satellites, real-time alerts, and a monthly subscription. The absurdity is the mismatch: military-grade tracking for laundry. The punchline lands on the oldest sock truth there is (it was in the basket all along) and then charges extra for it, which is exactly how a founder who means every word would monetise his own uselessness. One product, one function, one sentence, no second idea crowding it.
